There is a quiet rhythm to an empty lecture hall when everyone else has gone home for the day. We spent three hours trying to simplify a matrix decomposition that refused to commute.

By five o'clock, the sun had dropped below the level of the tall windows, casting long amber rectangles across the floorboards. The board was full of crossed-out indices and chalk dust. In the end, the identity was simple: a sign change we had overlooked on the third line.

Moments like this remind me why pen, paper, and slate still hold an irreplaceable place in theoretical work.
